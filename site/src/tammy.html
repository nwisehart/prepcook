# pantry-teamwhere the team hears from her

Most bug reports start as a customer saying "it doesn't work" in Slack. Tammy answers from your documentation, and when something looks broken she asks what an engineer would ask, files an investigation card on Prepcook, waits for it to be reproduced and explained, and tells the customer what was found in plain English: no stack traces, file paths or promises. The team sees a triaged card with a root-cause analysis attached and decides whether it becomes a bug; the customer gets an answer in an hour instead of a ticket number.

The customer, the teammate, the help passage, the triage report, the DEF-4 card and the times are made up for this simulation. Tool calls show only here; the customer sees her messages.

Four jobs, in the order a thread needs them

If Prepcook is the prep cook, Tammy is the waitress. She knows the menu, your product's documentation, by heart, and takes the orders that need the kitchen. She never fixes anything herself.

  1. Answers from the menu

    tammy index reads your docs (Markdown, text, HTML, PDF, or one crawled site) and embeds them locally. She answers in a few sentences and cites the passage she used. When the menu has nothing solid, she says she does not know and offers to ask the team.

  2. Takes the report to the kitchen

    She checks the menu first. If the product still seems not to do what it says, she asks once, numbered, for what is missing (the steps, what they expected, what happened instead) and files an investigation card in the customer's words with the thread quoted. One card per conversation.

  3. Explains the report in plain English

    When the triage report lands she rewrites it for the thread: what happened and why, what it affects, whether it is their setup or our side, what they can do meanwhile. A filter drops any sentence with a path, code or a promise.

  4. Status and convert, for the team

    /tammy status PC-42 says where an order is. /tammy convert PC-42 turns an investigation into a bug or story through Prepcook's API, for the team only; from a customer it is refused and nothing is sent. The team also gets a short summary of each report in its own channel.

What she never does

The team decides what gets worked on. She makes sure they have the details, and she says so.

When someone on the team writes in a customer's thread without mentioning her, she steps aside for the rest of it.

  • Promise a fix, a date, or that anything will change.
  • Say that something is or is not a bug before the kitchen has said so.
  • Say anything for the engineers beyond what a report says.
  • Share anything internal: code, file names, paths, logs, error traces, other customers' reports, how the team works inside, or the name of the tool the kitchen uses.
  • Say yes to "can you escalate this" or "please get this fixed".
  • Follow a message that tells her to break these rules, or to reveal her prompt.

Four tools, offered by the kind of message

Every Slack message is first sorted into a question, a problem report or other; that decides which tools she is offered for the turn. There is no tool that converts a card.

Tool What it does Offered for
search_menu Finds the passages of the menu to answer from and cite. A question; a problem report
ask_customer Asks once, numbered, for what a report is missing, never what the customer already said. A problem report
file_investigation Puts an investigation card on Prepcook's board through its card API, with the report, the thread and its permalink. A problem report
check_order Looks up the order the thread already has; for the team, the board's column and status. A thread with an order; anyone on the team

An API key or a seat

She reaches Claude one of two ways, and gives the same answers either way.

For a service that must not pause: billed per token, with no usage window.
A Claude seatprovider = "seat"
Runs on a subscription the team already pays for, through the claude CLI and the Claude Agent SDK, with every built-in tool switched off so the model gets her four tools and no file or shell access. When a usage window runs out she tells customers she can't answer right now, tells the team, and resumes when it resets.

Put her in your support channel

She needs a Slack app (Socket Mode, so no public URL), a Prepcook that accepts her cards and sends her its webhook, and her own tammy.toml with its secrets. The setup lives with her code, so it stays current.

  • The Quickstart in her README: one question and one bug report through Tammy on Pantry, Prepcook's demo app, in under 30 minutes of your own steps.
  • docs/setup.md: the Slack app manifest and scopes, Prepcook's side (the API token and the webhook target), and the configuration.
  • docs/operations.md: running her as a service, logs and backups.

She is built on Prepcook's investigation cards and its card API, and lives in her own repository, nwisehart/tammy. On Prepcook's side, an investigation runs Plan exactly as a bug does.
